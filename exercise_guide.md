# Exercise & Stretch Guide

How to perform every movement in your exercise plan. Each entry covers starting position, execution, and the most common mistakes to avoid.

**Your equipment:** Variable dumbbells 2–16 kg | Treadmill max 12 km/h | 5 kg weighted vest | 2 × 0.5 kg wrist weights. Where equipment affects technique or load, notes are included in the relevant entry.

---

## Exercises

---

### Cat-Cow

**Starting position:** On all fours — hands directly under shoulders, knees under hips. Spine neutral, gaze at the floor.

**Execution:**
1. **Cat:** Exhale and round your entire spine toward the ceiling. Tuck your chin to your chest and tuck your pelvis under.
2. **Cow:** Inhale and let your belly drop toward the floor. Lift your head and tailbone gently upward.
3. Move slowly and continuously between the two positions, one breath per movement.

**Common mistakes:**
- Moving only the neck or only the lower back — the movement should travel through the entire spine.
- Rushing. The slower you go, the more effective it is.

---

### Glute Bridge

**Starting position:** Lie flat on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the floor hip-width apart. Arms at your sides, palms down.

**Execution:**
1. Press your feet into the floor and squeeze your glutes.
2. Drive your hips straight up until your body forms a straight line from knees to shoulders.
3. Hold at the top for 1–2 seconds, squeezing hard.
4. Lower slowly — take 2 seconds to come back down.

**Common mistakes:**
- Pushing through the lower back instead of the glutes — the glutes should do the work.
- Feet too far from your body, which shifts load to the hamstrings.
- Letting the hips drop on one side — keep the pelvis level.

---

### Hip Circles

**Starting position:** Stand with feet hip-width apart, hands on hips.

**Execution:**
1. Draw large circles with your hips — forward, to the side, back, to the other side.
2. Keep your upper body as still as possible; the movement comes entirely from the hips.
3. Complete all reps in one direction, then reverse.

**Common mistakes:**
- Making small circles — go as wide as comfortable to actually mobilise the hip joint.
- Leaning your whole torso into the movement.

---

### Thoracic Rotation

**Starting position:** Sit cross-legged on the floor, or kneel. Place your hands lightly behind your head, elbows wide.

**Execution:**
1. Keeping your hips still and facing forward, rotate your upper body to the right as far as comfortable.
2. Pause briefly at the end range, then rotate to the left.
3. The movement comes from the mid-back (thoracic spine), not the lower back or hips.

**Common mistakes:**
- Rotating from the hips — lock them in place.
- Pulling the neck with the hands — hands are just resting there.

---

### Jumping Jacks

**Starting position:** Stand with feet together, arms at your sides.

**Execution:**
1. Jump both feet out to shoulder width while simultaneously raising both arms overhead.
2. Jump back to the starting position.
3. Maintain a light, springy landing — don't crash down on your heels.

**Common mistakes:**
- Landing stiffly — keep a soft bend in the knees on landing.
- Going too fast before warming up.

---

### Brisk March

**Starting position:** Standing, feet hip-width apart.

**Execution:**
1. March on the spot, lifting knees to hip height or as high as comfortable.
2. Swing opposite arms naturally — left arm with right knee.
3. Keep an upright posture; don't lean forward.

**Common mistakes:**
- Barely lifting the knees — drive them up to get the hip flexors working.

---

### Dumbbell Deadlift

**Starting position:** Stand with feet hip-width apart, a dumbbell in each hand resting at your thighs.

**Execution:**
1. Push your hips back (not down) and hinge forward, allowing the dumbbells to lower down the front of your thighs.
2. Keep your back flat, chest up, and the dumbbells close to your body throughout.
3. Lower until you feel a stretch in your hamstrings (roughly shin level), keeping the spine neutral.
4. Drive your hips forward to stand back up, squeezing the glutes at the top.

**Common mistakes:**
- Rounding the lower back — if this happens, reduce the range of motion until you're stronger and more flexible.
- Letting the dumbbells drift away from your body.
- Squatting down instead of hinging — the hips go back, not straight down.

---

### Goblet Squat

**Starting position:** Stand with feet slightly wider than hip-width, toes turned out 15–30°. Hold one dumbbell vertically at chest height with both hands cupping the top end.

**Execution:**
1. Brace your core and keep your chest up.
2. Push your knees out in the direction of your toes as you lower down.
3. Descend until your thighs are parallel to the floor or as low as comfortable.
4. Keep your heels flat on the floor throughout.
5. Drive through your heels to stand back up.

**Common mistakes:**
- Heels rising off the floor — indicates tight calves or ankles; reduce depth until mobility improves.
- Knees caving inward — actively push them out over your toes.
- Leaning forward excessively — the dumbbell as a counterweight should help with this.

---

### Single-Arm Dumbbell Row

**Starting position:** Place your left hand and left knee on a chair or bench for support. Hold a dumbbell in your right hand, arm hanging straight down, back flat and roughly parallel to the floor.

**Execution:**
1. Pull the dumbbell up toward your hip — not your shoulder — by driving your elbow back and up.
2. Squeeze your shoulder blade toward your spine at the top.
3. Lower slowly over 2–3 seconds back to the starting position.
4. Complete all reps on one side before switching.

**Common mistakes:**
- Pulling with the bicep rather than leading with the elbow.
- Rotating the torso to get the weight up — keep the back flat and still.
- Letting the dumbbell drop quickly — the lowering phase builds as much strength as the pull.

---

### Reverse Lunge

**Starting position:** Stand upright, feet together, a dumbbell in each hand (or bodyweight to start).

**Execution:**
1. Step one foot directly backward, landing on the ball of that foot.
2. Lower your back knee toward the floor — stop just before it touches.
3. Both knees should be at roughly 90° at the bottom.
4. Drive through the front heel to return to standing.
5. Alternate legs each rep, or complete all reps on one side then switch.

**Common mistakes:**
- Stepping too short, which forces the front knee far past the toes — step far enough back that the front shin stays mostly vertical.
- Letting the front knee cave inward — keep it tracking over your second toe.
- Leaning heavily forward — stay upright.

---

### Bird Dog

**Starting position:** On all fours — hands under shoulders, knees under hips. Back flat, gaze at the floor.

**Execution:**
1. Simultaneously extend your right arm forward and your left leg backward.
2. Both should reach parallel to the floor — no higher.
3. Hold for 2 seconds at the top, keeping the hips level (don't let one side hike up).
4. Return slowly and repeat with the opposite arm and leg.

**Common mistakes:**
- Letting the hips rotate or one hip hiking up — the pelvis stays completely level.
- Reaching too high with the arm or leg — stop at parallel.
- Rushing. Slow, controlled movement is the entire point.

---

### Farmer's Carry

**Starting position:** Stand upright holding a dumbbell in each hand (or one heavy dumbbell to the side). Arms at your sides.

**Execution:**
1. Stand tall — shoulders back and down, chest up, core braced.
2. Walk at a steady, controlled pace for the target duration.
3. Do not let the weight pull your shoulder down or your torso lean to one side.
4. Breathe steadily throughout.

**Common mistakes:**
- Shrugging the shoulders up toward the ears — shoulders stay down and back.
- Letting the lower back arch excessively — brace your core as if you expect a punch.
- Walking too fast — this is a strength exercise, not a sprint.

**With your equipment:** Start at 12 kg per hand. When this feels easy for 30 seconds, increase to 14 kg, then 16 kg. Once you hit the 16 kg ceiling, add the 5 kg vest — this raises your effective bodyweight to 95 kg and significantly increases the postural demand without changing the movement.

---

### Romanian Deadlift (RDL)

**Starting position:** Stand with feet hip-width apart, a dumbbell in each hand resting at the front of your thighs. Soft bend in the knees.

**Execution:**
1. Push your hips backward and lower the dumbbells down the front of your legs, keeping them in contact with your thighs.
2. Keep your back flat and your chest up throughout — this is a hip hinge, not a squat.
3. Lower until you feel a strong stretch in your hamstrings (usually mid-shin level).
4. Squeeze the glutes and drive the hips forward to return to standing.

**Common mistakes:**
- Bending the knees too much, turning it into a squat.
- Rounding the lower back — this is the most important mistake to avoid. If your back rounds, reduce the range of motion.
- Letting the dumbbells drift away from your legs.

*Note: The RDL is your single most important lower back rehab exercise. Treat it with care.*

---

### Dumbbell Floor Press

**Starting position:** Lie on your back on the floor, a dumbbell in each hand. Bring the dumbbells to your shoulders with elbows bent, upper arms resting on the floor at about 45° from your torso.

**Execution:**
1. Press both dumbbells straight up until your arms are fully extended above your chest.
2. Lower slowly until your upper arms touch the floor again — this is your bottom position.
3. Pause briefly, then press again.

**Common mistakes:**
- Flaring the elbows out to 90° — keep them at 45° to protect the shoulder joints.
- Bouncing the upper arms off the floor to generate momentum — touch and pause.
- Letting the dumbbells drift over your face rather than your chest.

---

### Bent-Over Dumbbell Row

**Starting position:** Hold a dumbbell in each hand. Hinge forward at the hips until your torso is at roughly 45° to the floor. Knees slightly bent, back flat, dumbbells hanging below your chest.

**Execution:**
1. Pull both dumbbells up toward your hips simultaneously, driving your elbows back and up.
2. Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the top.
3. Lower slowly over 2–3 seconds.

**Common mistakes:**
- Straightening up during the row to use momentum — stay hinged throughout.
- Pulling to the chest instead of the hips — this shifts to shoulder work rather than back.
- Rounding the lower back — brace your core before each rep.

---

### Dumbbell Shoulder Press

**Starting position:** Sit on a chair with back support (or stand). Hold a dumbbell in each hand at shoulder height, palms facing forward, elbows at roughly 90°.

**Execution:**
1. Press both dumbbells straight overhead until arms are fully extended.
2. Lower slowly back to shoulder height.

**Common mistakes:**
- Pressing forward rather than straight up — the dumbbells should end directly above your shoulders.
- Arching the lower back — especially common when standing. Brace the core.
- Letting the elbows flare too far forward at the bottom — keep them slightly in front of your body, not behind your ears.

---

### Dead Bug

**Starting position:** Lie flat on your back. Raise both arms straight toward the ceiling. Raise both knees to 90°, shins parallel to the floor (tabletop position).

**Execution:**
1. Slowly lower your right arm overhead toward the floor while simultaneously extending your left leg out straight, hovering just above the floor.
2. Return both to the starting position.
3. Repeat on the opposite side: left arm, right leg.
4. Keep your lower back pressed firmly into the floor throughout — this is the entire challenge.

**Common mistakes:**
- Letting the lower back arch off the floor — if this happens, reduce the range of motion of the arm and leg.
- Holding your breath — exhale as you extend.
- Moving too fast — the exercise only works if you move slowly and maintain the back-to-floor contact.

---

### Suitcase Carry

**Starting position:** Stand upright holding one dumbbell at your side in one hand, as if carrying a suitcase.

**Execution:**
1. Stand tall — resist any lean toward the weighted side.
2. Walk at a steady, controlled pace for the target duration.
3. Keep your shoulder on the weighted side from hiking up or dropping down.
4. Complete all reps on one side, then switch.

**Common mistakes:**
- Leaning away from the weight — the whole point is resisting that lean.
- Letting the shoulder hike up on the weighted side.
- Letting your gait become lopsided — walk as if you're carrying nothing.

**With your equipment:** Start at 12 kg. Progression path: 12 → 14 → 16 kg. When 16 kg for 30 sec is easy, switch to a 1-minute carry, or add the weighted vest for increased postural demand.

---

### Dumbbell Squat

**Starting position:** Stand with feet hip-width apart, toes slightly turned out. Hold a dumbbell in each hand at your sides.

**Execution:**
1. Brace your core and keep your chest up.
2. Push your hips back and bend your knees, lowering until thighs are parallel to the floor (or as low as comfortable).
3. Keep your heels flat on the floor and knees tracking over your toes.
4. Drive through your heels to stand back up.

**Common mistakes:**
- Heels lifting — indicates tight ankles or going too deep too soon.
- Knees caving inward — actively drive them outward.
- Looking down — keep your gaze forward to maintain an upright posture.

---

### Push-Up

**Starting position:** Hands on the floor slightly wider than shoulder-width, fingers pointing forward. Arms fully extended, body in a straight line from head to heels. Feet together or hip-width apart.

**Execution:**
1. Lower your chest toward the floor by bending your elbows, keeping them at roughly 45° from your torso.
2. Lower until your chest nearly touches the floor.
3. Push back up to the starting position.
4. Keep your body rigid throughout — no hips sagging or piking up.

**Modified version:** Perform with knees on the floor until you can complete 3 × 10 full push-ups.

**Common mistakes:**
- Hips sagging — squeeze your glutes and brace your core.
- Flaring elbows out to 90° — keep them at 45° for shoulder health.
- Only lowering partway — full range builds full strength.

**With your equipment (vest progression):** Once you can complete 3 × 15 clean push-ups, add the 5 kg vest. This adds ~5.5% to your effective bodyweight on a horizontal press, directly increasing chest, shoulder, and tricep load with no extra equipment needed. Do not add the vest until you can do 15 clean reps without it.

---

### Alternating Reverse Lunge

**Starting position:** Stand upright, feet together. Hands at sides or on hips.

**Execution:**
1. Step your right foot directly backward, landing on the ball of the foot.
2. Lower your right knee toward the floor.
3. Drive through the left heel to return to standing.
4. Immediately step the left foot back and repeat.
5. Continue alternating sides for the target rep count.

**Common mistakes:**
- Same as Reverse Lunge — step far enough back, stay upright, don't let the front knee cave.
- Losing balance during the transition — slow down if needed, there's no prize for speed here.

---

### Plank

**Starting position:** Forearms on the floor, elbows directly under shoulders. Body in a straight line from head to heels. Feet hip-width apart.

**Execution:**
1. Squeeze your glutes, brace your core, and press your forearms into the floor.
2. Hold the position, breathing steadily.
3. Keep your hips level — neither sagging nor piking.

**Common mistakes:**
- Hips sagging toward the floor — squeeze the glutes harder.
- Hips raised too high — lower them until your body is a straight line.
- Holding your breath — breathe consistently throughout.
- Looking up or down — keep a neutral neck, gaze at the floor slightly ahead of your hands.

**With your equipment (vest progression):** Once you can hold a 60-second plank with clean form, add the 5 kg vest. The vest sits across your back and increases the anti-gravity load on your core and shoulders without changing the movement pattern. Progression: 30 sec bodyweight → 60 sec bodyweight → 30 sec with vest → 60 sec with vest.

---

### Mountain Climbers

**Starting position:** Full push-up position — arms extended, hands under shoulders, body in a straight line.

**Execution:**
1. Drive your right knee toward your chest.
2. Quickly return it and simultaneously drive your left knee toward your chest.
3. Alternate at a steady, controlled pace — faster for more cardio, slower for more core focus.
4. Keep your hips level and your core braced throughout.

**Common mistakes:**
- Hips rising with each knee drive — keep them low and stable.
- Bouncing or losing rhythm — controlled alternation is more effective than frantic speed.
- Arms bending under fatigue — press the floor away, keep arms locked.

**With your equipment (vest progression):** Once you can complete 20 reps with clean form, add the 5 kg vest. Because this is a high-heart-rate exercise, the vest increases the cardiovascular demand significantly — expect your heart rate to spike 10–15 bpm higher with the vest on. Don't add it until the movement is fully automatic.

## Treadmill Protocols

---

### Treadmill Walk (Recovery / Non-training days)

**Purpose:** Active recovery, lower back mobility, cardiovascular base building, calorie burn without adding muscular fatigue.

**Starting position:** Step onto the belt before starting. Stand upright — don't hold the handles during the walk unless needed for safety. Set speed to target before fully committing your weight.

**Execution:**
1. Walk at an upright posture — don't lean forward into the console.
2. Allow your arms to swing naturally and oppositely (right arm with left foot).
3. Keep your gaze forward, not down at your feet.
4. Breathe through your nose if possible — this keeps the effort aerobic and sustainable.

**Speed and incline targets:**

| Phase        | Speed        | Notes                                                 |
|--------------|--------------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| Month 1–3    | 5.5–6.0 km/h | Brisk walk, conversational pace                       |
| Month 4–6    | 6.0–6.5 km/h | Increase speed to raise cardiovascular demand         |
| Month 7–12   | 6.5 km/h     | Sustained pace; vest adds progressive overload        |

**With vest and wrist weights:** Wearing the 5 kg vest + 0.5 kg wrist weights (total +6 kg) raises your effective weight to 96 kg. This increases calorie burn by ~20–25% and adds meaningful load to the glutes, core, and postural muscles — essentially turning a walk into a weighted carry. Introduce from month 3 during walks only, not jogging.

**Common mistakes:**
- Holding the handles — this unloads the core and changes posture. Only hold for safety.
- Leaning forward into the console — stand tall.
- Short, shuffling steps — walk with a full, natural stride.

---

### Treadmill Intervals (Day C stamina session)

**Purpose:** Build cardiovascular fitness and stamina by alternating between lower and higher intensity. More effective than steady-state for improving VO2max (your maximum aerobic capacity).

**Starting position:** Begin at warm-up walk speed. Gradually increase to the working speed when the interval starts — don't jump from 5.5 to 9 km/h in one step. Increase speed over 5–10 seconds.

**Execution:**
1. **Walk interval:** Maintain upright posture, breathe steadily, recover heart rate.
2. **Jog/run interval:** Lean very slightly forward from the ankles (not the waist), land mid-foot (not heel-first), arms at 90° swinging front-to-back (not crossing the body).
3. **Transition:** Smoothly increase or decrease speed over a few seconds — don't straddle the belt or grab the handles to change speed while running.
4. Complete the full interval table from your current phase — see the full protocol in the exercise plan.

**Target heart rate zones:**

| Zone         | BPM (approx. at 31)  | Feel                                  |
|--------------|----------------------|---------------------------------------|
| Warm-up walk | 90–110               | Easy, breathing normal                |
| Walk interval | 110–125             | Slightly elevated, can hold conversation |
| Jog interval | 130–155              | Breathing harder, can say a sentence  |
| Run interval | 155–175              | Difficult to speak more than a word   |

**With wrist weights during walks:** 0.5 kg per wrist during the walk recovery intervals adds light arm and shoulder endurance work. Remove them before jogging/running intervals — the added wrist weight disrupts natural arm swing at speed.

**Common mistakes:**
- Jogging at maximum speed before you can sustain it — 7–8 km/h is a solid beginner jog. Save 11–12 km/h for month 7+.
- Heel striking — especially common on treadmills. Aim to land mid-foot, directly under your centre of mass.
- Shortening stride at higher speeds — instead, increase cadence (steps per minute) rather than over-striding.
- Stopping abruptly after a run interval — always transition to a walk, never jump off a moving belt.

**Max speed context:** Your treadmill caps at 12 km/h. This is sufficient to reach Zone 4 (175 bpm) as a beginner and to run a pace of 5:00/km, which is a solid recreational fitness standard. You will not outgrow this treadmill for the first 2–3 years of training.

---

### Steady-State Treadmill Jog (Month 5+)

**Purpose:** Build sustained aerobic endurance — the ability to maintain effort for 20–30 minutes continuously. This is the foundation of stamina.

**Execution:**
1. Set speed to 8.0–9.0 km/h — a pace where you can speak a full sentence but it takes effort.
2. Maintain this pace for the full target duration (start with 15 min, build to 30 min over months).
3. Focus on relaxed form: loose shoulders, steady breathing, consistent cadence.
4. If you can't maintain the pace, slow down by 0.5 km/h rather than stopping.

**Progression:**

| Week     | Duration at 8.0–9.0 km/h |
|----------|---------------------------|
| Week 1–2 | 15 min continuous         |
| Week 3–4 | 18 min continuous         |
| Week 5–6 | 22 min continuous         |
| Week 7–8 | 25 min continuous         |
| Month 4+ | 30 min continuous         |

**Common mistakes:**
- Starting too fast — the first 5 minutes should feel almost too easy. You'll appreciate that conservatism at minute 20.
- Watching the timer — look forward or watch something. Checking every 30 seconds makes it feel much harder.

## Stretches & Cool-Down Movements

---

### Hip Flexor Stretch (Standing)

**Starting position:** Stand upright.

**Execution:**
1. Step your right foot forward into a lunge position, left knee lowered to the floor (or hovering above it for standing version).
2. Shift your weight forward slightly until you feel a stretch in the front of your left hip.
3. Keep your torso upright — don't lean forward.
4. Hold for 30 seconds, then switch sides.

**What you should feel:** A stretch deep in the front of the rear hip, not the knee.

---

### Hip Flexor Lunge Stretch

**Starting position:** From a kneeling lunge — right foot forward, left knee on the floor.

**Execution:**
1. Shift your weight gently forward over the right foot until you feel the stretch in the left hip flexor.
2. Optionally, raise your left arm overhead and lean slightly to the right to increase the stretch.
3. Hold for 30 seconds. Switch sides.

**What you should feel:** A deep stretch in the front of the left hip, potentially into the quad.

---

### Pigeon Pose

**Starting position:** From all fours, bring your right knee forward and out to the side, placing it behind your right wrist. Your right foot moves toward your left wrist. Extend your left leg straight back behind you.

**Execution:**
1. Lower your hips toward the floor — they may not touch; that is fine.
2. Stay upright on your hands, or for a deeper stretch, fold your torso forward and rest on your forearms or the floor.
3. Breathe deeply and let the hip relax with each exhale.
4. Hold for 30 seconds. Switch sides.

**What you should feel:** A deep stretch in the outer right hip and glute.

**Modification:** If this is too intense, lie on your back and cross your right ankle over your left knee (figure-four stretch) as an easier alternative.

---

### Child's Pose

**Starting position:** Kneel on the floor, big toes touching, knees hip-width apart or wider.

**Execution:**
1. Sit back toward your heels and fold your torso forward between your knees.
2. Extend your arms forward on the floor, or rest them alongside your body.
3. Let your forehead rest on the floor.
4. Breathe deeply, allowing the lower back to lengthen with each exhale.
5. Hold for 30 seconds.

**What you should feel:** A gentle decompression and lengthening of the lower back.

---

### Chest Doorframe Stretch

**Starting position:** Stand in a doorway. Place both forearms vertically on either side of the doorframe, elbows at shoulder height.

**Execution:**
1. Step one foot forward through the doorway.
2. Lean your body gently forward until you feel a stretch across the chest and front of the shoulders.
3. Hold for 30 seconds.

**What you should feel:** A stretch across the pectoral muscles and the front of the shoulders.

**Why it matters for you:** Desk posture causes the chest muscles (pec minor) to shorten and the shoulders to round forward. This directly reverses that pattern.

---

### Romanian Deadlift Stretch (Bottom hold)

**Starting position:** Same as the Romanian Deadlift, but with light or no weight.

**Execution:**
1. Hinge forward into the bottom position of the RDL.
2. At the point of maximum hamstring stretch, pause and hold.
3. Breathe deeply — on each exhale, let the hamstrings relax and allow a slight increase in range.
4. Hold for 20–30 seconds.

**What you should feel:** A strong, comfortable stretch through the hamstrings and a lengthening of the lower back.

---

### Seated Forward Fold

**Starting position:** Sit on the floor with legs extended straight out in front of you.

**Execution:**
1. Sit tall, then hinge forward from the hips — not by rounding the lower back.
2. Reach toward your feet or shins, going only as far as comfortable.
3. Hold the furthest point for 30 seconds, breathing steadily.
4. On each exhale, try to relax slightly deeper into the stretch.

**What you should feel:** A stretch through the hamstrings and, to a lesser extent, the lower back.

**Common mistakes:**
- Rounding aggressively from the lower back to reach further — go less far with a flatter back.
- Holding the breath — exhale into the stretch.

---

### Supine Knee-to-Chest Stretch

**Starting position:** Lie flat on your back.

**Execution:**
1. Pull one or both knees toward your chest, wrapping your hands around your shins.
2. Gently draw the knees in until you feel a comfortable stretch in the lower back.
3. Hold for 30 seconds.
4. For single leg: keep the other leg extended flat on the floor.

**What you should feel:** A gentle decompression and stretch in the lower back and glutes.

---

*Each exercise description is matched to the movements in your exercise plan. If any movement causes sharp or radiating pain — particularly in the lower back, knees, or shoulders — stop and substitute a lighter or modified version.*
